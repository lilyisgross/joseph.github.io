Professional Context

Perhaps because the educational infrastructure is less developed than in other LIS fields like archives or libraries, there is a very robust network of professional networks that audiovisual archivists can rely on for professional resources, including workshops, resource-shares, and opportunities for community more generally.

Two of the biggest professional associations include AMIA (the Association of Moving Image Archivists) and ARSC (the Association for Recorded Sound Collections). AMIA publishes The Moving Image journal. In the international A/V archives space, there is FIAF (the International Federation of Film Archives) which publishes the Journal of Film Preservation.

Jobs are posted in a variety of locations, including the AMIA job boarda, through local archivist collectives like the Los Angeles Archivists Collective (check out their newsletter, which has great resources!), via email ListServs (you can sign up for AMIA’s here), and sometimes through more general arts job boards, like Arts for LA.

We’d also recommend checking out groups like TAPE and the Magnetic Media Crisis Committee, which run their own discord servers.

Five Ways to Understand the Profession

Giovanna Fossati, From Grain to Pixel(First edition 2009) – widely considered the definitive book on film preservation, this text applies an ontological analysis to the processes of moving between analog and digital film. Drawing on both film theory, archival theory, and archival practice, Fossati offers a comprehensive the theory of film preservation, and looks at examples and case studies from the work of different film archives.

Diana Weynand & Marcus Weise, How Video Works(First edition 2004) – this textbook offers a comprehensive explanation of how analog video is encoded and transmitted in relatively user-friendly, non-technical language. It includes a glossary, many diagrams, and a comprehensive explanation of how pro and consumer video technology has changed over time, as well as some of the basic problems encountered in video preservation.

UCLA Library's Media Archival Studies Lib Guide – This guide contains selected resources for students in the UCLA Media Archive Studies MLIS specialization. Note that some publications and tools related to moving image preservation and access can be found online via organizational websites and pamphlets, rather than in academic press publications. This guide was created in 2023 by Diana King and Arts Library Research Assistant Diana Cardenas.

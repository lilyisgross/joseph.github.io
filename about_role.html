Typical Tasks

Below, we've listed some typical jobs in this subfield, ranging from entry level to managerial. At the bottom of each tier, we've also listed some comon expectations for each job

Entry Level

  1. Audiovisual Conservation Production Technician (Intern)

Tasks: reformatting A/V media; digital asset management

Intermediate

  1. Preservation Officer at the George Eastman Museum
  2. Audio Visual Preservation Specialist at the University of Kansas Libraries
  3. Film Technician at Northeast Historic Film (NHF)
  4. Audiovisual Preservation Project Manager at George Blood LP

Tasks: scanning film; color correction; maintain equipment (scanners, etc.); preservation quality control; communicate with external project stakeholders

Senior / Managerial

  1. Sr. Newsreel Preservationist at the UCLA Film & Television Archive (FTVA)
  2. Assistant Director for Media Preservation at NYU Libraries

Tasks: oversee preservation workflows; selection/appraisal for preservation priority; metadata quality control; knowledge of media production, broadcast TV, and cinema history

Job-Market Outlook

Per our conversation with Mark Toscano, while the number of traditional film preservation roles are shrinking (and there were unfortunately never many film archivists to begin with), there has been an expanded need for trained A/V media archivists in the LIS professions and GLAM institutions. Those filling more traditional LIS roles like metadata specialist, processing archivist, or cataloger are now sometimes expected to be able to handle audiovisual materials, or institutions may set aside a specific role to handle A/V material processing (e.g., digital preservation specialist, A/V cataloger, etc.).

In 2020, AMIA began conducting an “Annual Salary & Demographics Survey.” The first one was published in The Moving Image in 2020, and you can read it here. It’s not quite annual (they just solicited responses for a third edition, in 2025), but hopefully a new one will be released soon.

Training Agenda

There are very few programs that offer formal training in film and A/V archiving. There are a few other programs exclusively focused in this area: the film preservation certificate at the Selznick School in Rochester, NY, and NYU’s Moving Image Archiving Program. UCLA’s MLIS is one of the only MLIS programs in the country to include a specialization in media archives, and outside of attending a graduate program dedicated to media preservation, is one of the best degree routes to gaining such experience. UCLA did at one time offer a degree in Moving Image Archiving Studies (more similar to NYU’s MIAS program) run jointly with the School of Film & Television, but this was folded into the MLIS.

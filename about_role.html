Typical Tasks

Below, we've listed some typical jobs in this subfield, ranging from entry level to managerial. At the bottom of each tier, we've also listed some comon expectations for each job

Entry Level

  1. Audiovisual Conservation Production Technician (Intern)

Tasks: reformatting A/V media; digital asset management

Intermediate

  1. Preservation Officer at the George Eastman Museum
  2. Audio Visual Preservation Specialist at the University of Kansas Libraries
  3. Film Technician at Northeast Historic Film (NHF)
  4. Audiovisual Preservation Project Manager at George Blood LP

Tasks: scanning film; color correction; maintain equipment (scanners, etc.); preservation quality control; communicate with external project stakeholders

Senior / Managerial

  1. Sr. Newsreel Preservationist at the UCLA Film & Television Archive (FTVA)
  2. Assistant Director for Media Preservation at NYU Libraries

Tasks: oversee preservation workflows; selection/appraisal for preservation priority; metadata quality control; knowledge of media production, broadcast TV, and cinema history

Training Agenda

There are very few programs that offer formal training in film and A/V archiving. There are a few other programs exclusively focused in this area: the film preservation certificate at the Selznick School in Rochester, NY, and NYU’s Moving Image Archiving Program.

UCLA’s MLIS is one of the only MLIS programs in the country to include a specialization in media archives, and outside of attending a graduate program dedicated to media preservation, is one of the best degree routes to gaining such experience. UCLA did at one time offer a degree in Moving Image Archiving Studies (more similar to NYU’s MIAS program) run jointly with the School of Film & Television, but this was folded into the MLIS. The Media Archival Studies-focused courses offered by UCLA in the 2025-2026 academic year are INF STD 484: Audio Archiving, INF STD 480: Introduction to Media Archiving and Preservation, and INF STD 481: Moving Image Media and Technologies. Resources and syllabi from UCLA MAS years past as well as from the MIAS program are available online for further study. See former UCLA MLIS director Dr. Snowden Becker’s course descriptions and individual syllabi, as well as MIAS core readings (2012-2013) and MIAS core resources (2012-2013).

Outside of specialized programs, internships, volunteer work, and undertaking personal projects are a great way to build hands-on, practical skills. We highly recommend interested students check out the IS Dept.’s Media Preservation Lab, which has state-of-the-art digitization equipment and can accommodate a range of different film, analog video, and analog audio formats! You can book time with the equipment here, and there is no need for prior experience – you can book time in order to learn how to use the equipment.

Overall, students should focus on gaining a range of traditional LIS skills (metadata, cataloging, description, collections processing, etc.), alongside media archive-specific skills, such as how to use a film scanner, or how to generate inspection reports on analog A/V media.
